One continuous shot, no cuts. Real footage filmed on a cinema camera locked off on a tripod, perfectly still, inside an old colonial Mexican church nave at night: stone piers, rows of dark wooden pews with white roses tied to their ends, gilded carved altarpiece with saint statues, a red neon cross above it. A few people sit quietly in the pews and a woman in a white lace veil sits at the right. In the right foreground a black loudspeaker stands at the end of the pews, an acoustic guitar leans against it and an old upright double bass stands beside it. On top of the loudspeaker a clay incense bowl with small burning copal flames and a lit votive candle in red glass; more votive candles on the pews. Everything stays perfectly still and unchanged: the people, the carved altarpiece, the saints, the roses, the instruments and their strings. Only the flames move, swaying softly and lazily, never jumping or flaring. A thin ribbon of copal smoke drifts upward from the clay bowl toward the altar, unhurried and calm, curling lazily as it rises, always the same density, with no gusts and no sudden changes; the votive candles give off only faint, quiet wisps. Nothing hurries. The red neon cross glows steadily. Low key, deep black shadows, fine film grain. Nothing else moves. Audio is ambient sound only: quiet room tone.
