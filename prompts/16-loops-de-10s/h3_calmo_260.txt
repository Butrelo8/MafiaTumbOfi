One continuous shot, no cuts. Real footage filmed on a cinema camera locked off on a tripod, perfectly still, inside an old colonial Mexican church nave at night: stone piers, rows of dark wooden pews, gilded carved altarpiece with saint statues, a red neon cross above it. In the right foreground an old upright double bass leans against the first pew, and on the pew seat a small acoustic guitar leans back against a black portable loudspeaker. On the loudspeaker a small clay incense burner with glowing copal and a lit votive candle; more votive candles on the pews. Everything stays perfectly still and unchanged: the carved altarpiece, the saints, the instruments and their strings. Only the candle flames move, swaying softly and lazily, never jumping or flaring. A thin ribbon of copal smoke drifts upward from the clay incense burner, unhurried and calm, curling lazily as it rises, always the same density, with no gusts and no sudden changes; the votive candles give off only faint, quiet wisps. Nothing hurries. The red neon cross glows steadily. Low key, deep black shadows, fine film grain. Nothing else moves. Audio is ambient sound only: quiet room tone.
