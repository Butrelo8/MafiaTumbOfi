One continuous shot, no cuts. Real footage filmed on a cinema camera inside an old colonial Mexican church nave at night: stone piers, rows of dark wooden pews with white roses tied to their ends, gilded carved altarpiece with saint statues, a red neon cross above it. A few people sit quietly in the pews and a woman in a white lace veil sits at the right. In the right foreground a black loudspeaker stands at the end of the pews, an acoustic guitar leans against it and an old upright double bass stands beside it; on top of the loudspeaker a clay incense bowl of glowing copal embers and a lit votive candle in red glass. The people, the instruments and their strings stay perfectly still, solid and unchanged. The camera trucks smoothly to the right past the instruments and passes close behind a dark stone pier that fills the whole frame, turning the image black. The lighting never changes: the stone floor stays dark and in shadow, the moonlight stays in its shafts. The candle flames flicker gently and thin copal incense smoke drifts slowly through the warm candlelight. The red neon cross glows steadily. Low key, deep black shadows, fine film grain. Audio is ambient sound only: quiet room tone.
