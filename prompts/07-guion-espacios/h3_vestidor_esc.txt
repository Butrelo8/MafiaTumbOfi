One continuous shot, no cuts. Real footage filmed on a cinema camera locked off on a tripod, perfectly still, inside the vestry of an old colonial Mexican church at night, now holding the stage clothes of a corridos tumbados band: an old whitewashed lime plaster wall with a red ochre wainscot, a tall mirror in a carved gilded frame flanked by two brass wall sconces with candles, a dark carved wooden dresser below it with three lit votive candles in red glass, a light wooden acoustic guitar resting on a wooden chair, an open dark wooden wardrobe with stage jackets on brass hangers. Everything stays perfectly still and unchanged: the mirror and its reflection, the wardrobe, the clothes, the guitar and its strings. Only the candle flames move, swaying softly and lazily, never jumping or flaring, and their warm glow breathes gently on the nearby surfaces. The reflections of the votive flames on the varnished dresser top flicker with them. Nothing hurries. Low key, deep black shadows, fine film grain. Nothing else moves. Audio is ambient sound only: quiet room tone.
